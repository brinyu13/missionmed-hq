# I1Q-3000 Design Evolution Report

## Executive finding

The recovered history is not one straight product lineage. It is four overlapping streams:

1. **Direct Dr. J question lineage:** legacy JBank → video-synchronized VDRL → I1Q-2000 Ladder → I1Q-2001 Daily Drills integration → I1Q-2002 Ladder/Rounds bakeoff.
2. **Daily Drills interaction lineage:** T-12 visual breadth → T-13 truth correction → T-14 simplification/mobile → T-15 component and discovery studies → T-16 synthesis.
3. **Question-platform lineage:** I1Q-1002 architecture → I1Q-1004C entity/security amendment → I1Q-1008B Question Factory/Library requirements → current 17-workflow internal studio.
4. **Adjacent pattern donors:** QBANK, STAT, Grand Rounds, Timeline Builder, and CAM. These contribute interaction, review, provenance, motion, and visual patterns; they are not direct Dr. J Learning Studio ancestors.

No inspected artifact is a ratified Learning Studio product. No exact `Learning Studio` implementation was recovered. The strongest local evidence is still prototype, requirements, local-candidate, historical, or adjacent-reference evidence.

## Authority and truth boundary

- I1Q-3000 is supplied-ticket scope and is not registered in the inspected MissionMed mission registry.
- This report performs discovery and comparison only. It does not build, merge, redesign, deploy, or adopt.
- “Canonical design inventory” in this package means a normalized evidence catalog. It does not mean canonical product design.
- Dates are approximate local file modification dates. They support relative sequencing but do not independently prove authorship, approval, or first creation.
- Authentic drill question text, learner names, raw transcripts, and restricted export content are not reproduced.
- A filename containing `production`, `canonical`, `definitive`, or `live` is not treated as authority.

## What became stronger

### 1. Truthfulness of the learning interaction

T-12’s server-graded MCQ assumption gave way to T-13’s timed spoken/open recall and self-report. T-14 simplified that contract without pretending the system could grade a spoken response. T-16 then carried the truthful interaction into a coherent browse/session/runtime/summary shell.

This is one of the clearest improvements in the entire corpus: the interface progressively matched what the learner was actually doing.

### 2. Source provenance and replay

The legacy JBank paired educational forms but had no source occurrence, timestamp, or replay contract. VDRL-090 introduced video/node synchronization. I1Q-2001 then made source-first identity, exact time anchors, replay entry points, and authentic-versus-generated status central to the design.

The current Question Platform adds the missing governance side: immutable source occurrence → item revision → release lineage, with privacy, rights, and review state. It does not yet connect that contract to a protected learner runtime.

### 3. Educational progression

The legacy paired checkpoint/vignette pattern became an explicit transfer model in I1Q-2000:

- Dr. J ask;
- foundational recall;
- board framing;
- clinical vignette;
- convergence;
- calibration and next action.

I1Q-2002 added a different strength: a continuous clinical thread in which a fact drives consequence, board reasoning, deeper questioning, and a decision. That thread is a meaningful educational experiment even though it is mostly authored and remains physician-gated.

### 4. Mode honesty

Across the corpus, clearer mode contracts emerged:

- Practice versus Test in legacy JBank;
- Learning versus Exam versus Review in Ladder;
- full versus review intent in Daily Rounds;
- open-recall self-report in Daily Drills;
- multiple response modes and live adjudication in Grand Rounds.

The strongest later artifacts make feedback timing, answer visibility, and authority explicit rather than blending incompatible modes.

### 5. Debrief and remediation

Legacy results/topic summaries evolved into richer models:

- Ladder calibration and blind spots;
- I1Q-2001 Review Bay and exact-moment replay;
- STAT natural replay and session review;
- Grand Rounds Chart Review, error classification, next actions, and separated score/mastery/readiness concepts.

The Grand Rounds separation is especially important as a truth pattern: competitive score, medical mastery, and exam readiness are different claims and must remain different.

### 6. Motion discipline

Grand Rounds 2519X exposed the failure mode: scene picker, Continue buttons, feedback covering the clue, and motion explained by title cards. Grand Rounds 2522F materially improved the model with one persistent stage, tile-to-clue continuity, lower-third verdicts, staged signal failure, phone control, focus contracts, and reduced-motion information holds.

The reusable lesson is narrow: motion can carry state and continuity. It should not merely decorate a transition or obscure educational content.

### 7. Responsive and accessibility evidence

T-14 introduced explicit phone-frame comparison. I1Q-2001 improved semantic and keyboard scaffolding. STAT 914B preserved a large multi-viewport and high-zoom evidence set. Grand Rounds 2522F documented roving focus, modal/focus behavior, and reduced-motion contracts.

No recovered Learning Studio artifact has complete accessibility certification. The progress is real, but the gate remains open.

## What became weaker or regressed

### 1. I1Q-2001 weakened the performed Ladder

I1Q-2000 required a learner response and confidence judgment at each rung, then supplied per-rung feedback, expandable L2/L3 explanation, replay, and convergence. I1Q-2001’s embedded Ladder is largely a passive snapshot. It gains ecosystem fit but loses part of the experiential proof.

I1Q-2001 also retains confidence capture while dropping I1Q-2000’s calibration and blind-spot debrief, and its “save/resume” state is session-memory only.

### 2. I1Q-2002 used a weakened Ladder comparator

This is the most important archaeological correction.

I1Q-2002’s Model A asks one recall question and then becomes a watched ascent. It does **not** reproduce I1Q-2000’s full three-rung answer/confidence/feedback interaction. Therefore the historical Rounds verdict rejects the passive comparator implemented in I1Q-2002; it does not establish rejection of the strongest recovered Ladder.

The verdict is also P3 design evidence, not Founder ratification or production authority.

### 3. Longitudinal learning signals disappeared

I1Q-2000 proposed calibration, blind spots, mastery, and season-like continuity. Later I1Q prototypes do not preserve the whole longitudinal model. Some of the labels were speculative, but the underlying need—persistent, evidence-qualified remediation over time—remains unresolved.

### 4. Learner UI exposed implementation plumbing

I1Q-2001 includes pending-generation, hydration, and contract language in learner-facing states. That improves prototype truthfulness for reviewers but increases learner cognitive load. The archaeological conclusion is not to hide provenance; it is to separate operational status from learner instruction while preserving inspectable evidence.

### 5. Simplification sometimes lost useful uncertainty

T-13’s richer self-report vocabulary could distinguish uncertainty and out-of-time conditions. T-14’s Correct/Missed reduction is faster and cleaner but loses some metacognitive signal. Both ideas belong in the inventory because the tradeoff was never conclusively adjudicated.

### 6. Complexity outpaced benefit in several adjacent systems

- STAT added wagers, streaks, opponent pressure, lifelines, cursor/chase, and dense lifecycle state faster than it added explanation or Dr. J provenance.
- Grand Rounds 2601F added a setup wizard, several response modes, a host console, Final ritual, and seven postgame tabs. Some are excellent evidence; together they create a high operational burden.
- Timeline 407F expanded to eleven tabs and dense desktop tooling. Its provenance and versioning patterns are valuable, but its entire editor is not a learner-facing donor.

## Ideas that disappeared but should remain inspectable

These are preservation findings, not a product selection:

- Per-rung confidence-before-verdict.
- Calibration and blind-spot reporting.
- Expandable explanation depth and distractor rationale.
- The fully interactive convergence demonstration from I1Q-2000.
- Exact-time replay from every remediation surface.
- Authentic open-recall controls: lock-in, pace, pause, flag, out-of-time, and flagged review.
- Content-class labels at each authentic versus authored surface.
- I1Q-2001 block pacing, Exam navigator, mark/strike controls, confirmation, and Review Bay.
- VDRL’s node-synchronized video context and local note/export pattern.
- T-16’s browse/session/runtime/summary continuity.
- Timeline’s derived-question-to-source highlighting, candidate quarantine, visibility scopes, and version compare.
- Grand Rounds’ brief attending pearl, error classification, next-action rationale, and separation of score/mastery/readiness.

## Ideas that never worked well

- Server-graded fiction for an open spoken recall activity.
- Forty-card scrolling as the only large-drill navigation model.
- Unvalidated “IRT-inspired” scoring or keyword-inferred topic authority.
- Treating XP, coins, levels, game score, or streak as medical mastery.
- Mandatory ceremony on every question in a 150–220 occurrence drill.
- Permanent director rails and scene pickers as learner navigation.
- Continue/Next gates between every show beat.
- Feedback overlays that cover the question or clue.
- Decorative motion that carries no information.
- Authored clinical chains presented as authentic teaching without per-link status and physician review.
- “Replay” controls without a verified `video_id`, time anchor, privacy/rights state, and media source.
- Production/canonical conclusions based on filenames, local success, or a historical design verdict.

## Educational ideas that survived every generation

The common educational spine is stable even when the UI changes:

1. Retrieve a concise fact or principle.
2. Commit before seeing the answer.
3. Receive feedback after commitment.
4. Connect the fact to a clinical or board-style consequence.
5. Return to the original teaching context when confused.
6. Review errors and decide what to do next.

The corpus disagrees about whether the transfer should be a visible ladder, a continuous rounds thread, a paired checkpoint/vignette, or a game-show pearl. It does not disagree about the need to move beyond passive reading.

## Visual language findings

- CAM Concept D is the strongest recovered design-system donor, but it does not determine the learning interaction.
- I1Q-2000/2001’s dark assessment shell, restrained ember/gold accents, and visible progression remain coherent for focused retrieval.
- T-14/T-16 are stronger references for simplification and mobile drill flow.
- Grand Rounds is strongest where stage, motion, and feedback furniture clarify state; it is weakest where ceremony or trade dress dominates.
- Timeline is a strong operator/provenance reference, not a learner visual template.

No single visual language is selected by this report.

## Architecture reality

The locally observed topology remains disconnected:

```text
source occurrences / Gold Set
  -> internal Question Platform revision/release
  -> internal Drills adapter (feature-flagged off)
  -> protected learner consumer wiring not established

Daily selector
  -> drill registry and selected-drill launch payload
  -> protected Drills player

I1Q-2000/2001/2002
  -> standalone prototype data and in-memory state
  -> no verified protected-runtime adoption
```

Replay requires a verified media join (`video_id`, timestamp, source/revision hash, rights/privacy state, and playback availability). Zoom Notes are a separate registry sidecar, not interchangeable with local learner-authored Question Notes. Analytics remain separate among prototype memory, Daily local history, Drills responses, and Question Platform governance entities.

## Concepts that deserve to survive implementation review

The following are the strongest cross-generation preservation candidates. This is an inventory, not a final direction:

- occurrence/source identity and authentic-versus-authored labeling;
- full-breadth authentic recall plus bounded selective depth;
- confidence before verdict;
- layered, concise-first explanations;
- exact-moment replay with rights/privacy state;
- open-recall self-report with pause/flag/out-of-time behavior;
- clear Learning/Exam/Review contracts;
- error-classified debrief and one defensible next action;
- separated game score, mastery evidence, and readiness claims;
- immutable source → revision → release provenance;
- derived-question-to-source highlighting and version compare;
- motion that carries state and preserves information under reduced motion;
- responsive, keyboard-operable navigation with no question-covering feedback.

Founder review, physician review, privacy/rights confirmation, runtime architecture, and fresh accessibility validation remain separate future gates.
