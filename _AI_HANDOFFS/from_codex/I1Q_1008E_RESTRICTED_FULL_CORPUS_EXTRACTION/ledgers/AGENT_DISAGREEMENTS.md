# I1Q-1008E Agent Disagreements and Resolutions

## Recorded disagreements

1. **Persistent HMAC aliases versus protected random mapping.** Lorentz proposed or evaluated a persistent HMAC basis. The proposal was vetoed because a durable key would create secret custody, rotation, and leakage obligations. A protected random alias map without a reusable key was adopted.
2. **Legacy evidence as promotion authority versus secondary comparison.** The permissive interpretation was rejected. Legacy-derived material remains secondary and cannot promote unsupported transcript content, resolve missing evidence, or prove historical completeness.
3. **Automated pass completion versus specialist verification.** Automated PASS7/PASS8 proposals were not accepted as independent review. The final contract requires four distinct specialist reviewers, 194 verification cells, 388 role reviews, and an authoritative finalizer join.
4. **Initial classifier output versus independent medical/privacy review.** OSLER and assessment review challenged the automated nonmedical classification. The bounded 168-row correction and 2-row privacy-precedence repair were accepted; uncontrolled classifier widening was rejected.
5. **Process metadata locking versus kernel-backed exclusion.** Independent rotation audit rejected process-identity assumptions and pathname-only locking. Stable kernel-lock identity, immediate pre-mutation assertions, and shared exclusion were adopted.
6. **Overwrite-capable publication versus no-clobber publication.** Builder convenience was rejected after adversarial review. Exclusive publication, collision failure, synchronization, and byte-identical replay became mandatory.
7. **Seven-file correction evidence versus dependency-closed trust base.** Dependency audit found the original set incomplete. The trust base expanded to 11 exact files before live correction evidence was accepted.
8. **Generic specialist receipts versus finalizer authority binding.** Independent finalizer audit showed that a self-consistent receipt could bypass aggregate authority. The legacy assembler was disabled and receipts were bound to role batches, submissions, evidence, packet sets, and completion.
9. **Implementation privacy precedence versus stale schema rule.** The implementation correctly quarantined two privacy rows, while the schema still forced nonmedical rejection. The partial run failed at 95 of 97 and was archived; the schema was corrected rather than weakening privacy precedence.
10. **Help request expectation versus CLI live-mode default.** A `--help` invocation unexpectedly began a read-only live leakage scan. The process was contained before completion, no protected state changed, and explicit zero-I/O help plus regression coverage resolved the disagreement.
11. **Order-sensitive safe projection versus semantic roster equality.** The finalizer rejected an equivalent safe roster permutation. The resolution validates authoritative positions and exact semantics first, then sorts the copied projection; tampered positions and totals still fail.
12. **Additive compatibility versus strict re-extraction after finalizer drift.** Compatibility was rejected because finalizer bytes participate in the run contract. The system failed closed, completed a second non-destructive supersession, and performed a fresh extraction and four-role semantic rebind.

## Builder/audit accountability

Several material defects originated in builder implementations and were caught by independent audits before final reliance: lock-path replacement exposure, overwrite-capable publication, insufficient recovery authority binding, incomplete trust-base hashing, generic specialist-receipt acceptance, stale privacy schema logic, unsafe CLI help parsing, and order-sensitive safe-roster comparison. Each finding produced a bounded repair, focused regression coverage, and re-audit. No defect was waived into completion.

## Final resolution

All listed disagreements are resolved for this mission. Final evidence records 97 of 97 artifacts, 873 automated cells, 194 specialist cells, 388 role reviews, one durable aggregate plus two session-observed identical integration successes, and zero leakage findings. Product release and clinical/final-assessment authority remain outside scope.
