# V3 — Independent Pedagogy Verification (B1-513 Inspiration)

Verifier: independent fresh-context reviewer (did not build this) · Date: 2026-08-07
Scope: RESEARCH_NOTES.md, PROMPT_LIBRARY.json, B1-513_INSPIRATION_PRODUCT_AND_PEDAGOGY.md, B1-513_INSPIRATION_RESEARCH_APPENDIX.md, prototype/extensions.js (`renderInspiration`, `b1513WizardStepMarkup`, wizard handlers), prototype/shim.js (`/api/inspiration*`).
Question: will the Inspiration system actually broaden autobiographical recall and produce interview-usable material for residency applicants (incl. IMGs)?

**Verdict: CONFIRMED-WITH-FINDINGS.** The research base is real and accurately characterized (7 of 7 spot-checked sources verified, including every source the brief named). The 81-prompt bank is original, episodically cued, IMG-safe, and its 27-cell coverage claim verifies computationally to the exact numbers published. The safety design (in-text exits, no trauma demands, no diagnosis framing) holds under adversarial reading. The material gaps are on the *conversion* side: the one mechanism that turns recalled scenes into stakes-and-change interview material (the follow-up probe) is broken for typed answers in the reference implementation, and the claimed multi-stage deepening scaffold is in reality a single static probe with the `interviewUse` rationale never surfaced to anyone.

Findings: **0 × P0 · 2 × P1 · 8 × P2**

## P1 findings

### P1-1 — The follow-up probe never appears for typed answers in the reference implementation
**Where:** `prototype/extensions.js` — markup line 446, input handler lines 1224–1232.
**Evidence:** The question card renders the follow-up only when `insp.answer` is non-empty at render time:
```js
${insp.answer && prompt.followUp ? `<p class="b1513FollowUp">↳ ${esc(prompt.followUp)}</p>` : ''}
```
But the `input` handler for `[data-b1513-answer]` only updates `insp.answer` and toggles the Add-to-Library button — it never calls `b1513RerenderWizard()` (deliberately, to avoid destroying textarea focus). No other code path re-renders the card while a typed answer exists. Consequence: for the primary input path (typing), the follow-up **never displays**. It appears only after a *voice* recording stops (`b1513StopInspRecorder(true)` re-renders). 
**Why P1:** The product doc names `extensions.js` the "working reference implementation" and §3 promises "It is hidden until the student has begun answering, then appears under the question." Acceptance gate 5 ("follow-up appears only after the answer box is non-empty") would technically pass while the feature is functionally absent. The `followUp` is the *only* runtime mechanism implementing P7 (scene deepening) and the main nudge toward P10 (change/stakes) — with it silently missing, the wizard's pedagogical depth degrades to "answer one question and move on."
**Fix:** re-render (or directly inject) the follow-up node on first non-empty input without rebuilding the textarea; add an explicit acceptance test "type one character → follow-up visible."

### P1-2 — Stakes-and-change conversion is materially thinner than the documents claim
**Where:** product doc §8 rows P7/P10; PROMPT_LIBRARY.json `followUp`/`interviewUse` fields; entire prototype UI.
**Evidence, three converging gaps:**
1. **Claimed staged scaffold vs. single probe.** Product doc P7: "The `followUp` field applies the scene scaffold (place → senses → thought/feeling → meaning) to every prompt, one probe at a time after the first answer." Each prompt carries exactly **one** static follow-up; there is no staged sequence, no second probe, and no meaning-stage question for prompts whose single probe stops at the senses (e.g., q-001 "What does that dish taste like when it's made exactly right?", q-002 "What could you see and hear in that scene?"). A four-stage scaffold cannot be "applied" by one fixed sentence.
2. **`interviewUse` is invisible at runtime.** `grep interviewUse prototype/*.js` → zero matches. The field that "names the change/insight it yields" (P10 row) is shown to no student, no admin, nowhere. It is authoring metadata only; the student never learns *why* their dish memory is interview-relevant, and nothing in the wizard asks "what was at stake / what changed."
3. **A visible subset of light prompts has no change vector at all** (q-004, q-046, q-059, q-060 follow-ups deepen atmosphere, not shift — see weakest-prompts list).
**Consequence:** the wizard reliably *broadens recall* (the cue design is sound and evidence-true) but its unaided output trends toward pleasant reminiscence; conversion into interview material (stakes + change) is delegated entirely to downstream Library machinery (Learning Lesson, mentor review) that the Inspiration surface never mentions at answer time. The pedagogy documents overstate what the shipped mechanism does.
**Fix options (any one materially helps):** show `interviewUse` as a "why this works in an interview" reveal after answering; add a second-stage meaning probe ("What did that change?") before promotion; or seed the promoted story's Learning Lesson field from a change-question asked at promotion time.

## P2 findings

### P2-1 — Arithmetic errors in published coverage numbers (both directions)
- Appendix §5: "38 territories carry exactly one prompt" — **actual: 34** (53 distinct territories; distribution: 34×1, 12×2, 5×3, 2×4). Computed from the JSON.
- Product doc §8 P12: "46 of 81 prompts center someone other than the student" — **actual: 48** (prompts tagged `family` and/or `someone_else`: 24 + 25 − 1 shared (q-057) = 48; identical count for "who ≠ exactly ['you']").
Neither error changes the claim's direction, but a verification-grade appendix should not contain numbers that fail recomputation. Everything else recomputed **exactly**: who 35/24/25, domain 34/23/24, energy 27/31/24, all eight whoDetail counts, multi-tag lists (q-012, q-057, q-078 dual-who; q-024 dual-energy), largest-territory list, and the full 27-cell matrix (see Verified section).

### P2-2 — Two prompts sit close to well-circulated question stock
- **q-035** "What's a story your family tells about you that you'll never live down? Tell it the way they tell it." vs StoryCorps Great Questions: *"Are there any funny stories your family tells about you that come to mind?"* Not verbatim, and the episodic instruction is original, but the core clause ("stor(y/ies) your family tells about you") is nearly identical to a famous StoryCorps question. Given the repeated, emphatic "No StoryCorps questions were reproduced" stance, reword the core clause (e.g., anchor on the retelling occasion) to keep the claim unassailable.
- **q-004** "What's the hardest you can remember laughing?" is a near-match for the widely circulated generic icebreaker "What's the hardest you've ever laughed?" (Quora threads, conversation-prompt newsletters). Not owned by any bank, so no attribution problem — but it undercuts the "MissionMed-original" flavor and is also pedagogically weak (below).
No other verbatim or near-verbatim matches were found across the 15-prompt sample (methodology in Verified section).

### P2-3 — The five weakest prompts
1. **q-059** (friend / single look / "What's one running joke between you that outsiders would never get?") — the follow-up solicits material that is *definitionally untellable to an interviewer* (an outsider). Produces warm reminiscence with近-zero conversion; interviewUse ("raw material of team chemistry") is asserted, not mechanized.
2. **q-060** (best meal shared with a friend) — pure scene nostalgia; follow-up ("What were you two talking about?") adds atmosphere, not stakes; interviewUse ("small-talk-to-substance material") is the bank's most hand-wavy.
3. **q-004** (hardest laughing) — generic icebreaker (P2-2); humor stories are the classic "you had to be there" failure mode in interviews; no change vector anywhere in prompt, follow-up, or plausible answer.
4. **q-046** ("Who in your family 'helped' with your schoolwork… Whose patience ran out first?") — quietly assumes a family that *could* help with schoolwork; first-generation students (a MissionMed-relevant population) may have had no one, making the prompt land as exclusion rather than cue. Skip exists, but this is the one prompt where the "assume no particular family structure" screen has a blind spot (educational, not structural). Conversion also thin.
5. **q-073** (unsent update to a mentor; follow-up "What's stopped you from sending that update — and what if you did?") — the follow-up presumes the mentor is alive and reachable; for a student whose formative mentor has died, this is the bank's most likely accidental-grief moment, and it carries no in-text exit (it is `moving`). Also the frame is a hypothetical letter, not an episode — the scene only arrives via the sub-clause "which moment would you thank them for."
(Honorable mentions: q-015 is feeling-cued rather than event-cued — "a moment when you felt truly at home" is a mood search, the weakest retrieval cue in the bank and wistful territory for displaced IMGs; q-017's lucky-ritual frame yields charming but low-yield material.)

### P2-4 — The episodic warm-up (P3) is claimed in the library metadata but does not exist in the flow
`PROMPT_LIBRARY.json` meta `recommended_flow`: "Open each session with one low-stakes recent-moment warm-up to induce episodic specificity (Madore & Schacter)." The wizard has **no warm-up step of any kind**, does not default the ENERGY choice to light, and implements the light→serious→moving ramp purely as helper copy plus the de-escalation switch. The product doc is honest about this (§8 P3: "R4 candidate, not in R3"), but the library metadata asserts a flow the product does not have — and P3 is the single most mechanically supported broadening intervention in the whole evidence base. Additionally, the induction evidence is slightly over-extended: the lab induction (Madore & Schacter 2016, QJEP) probes a just-watched video and measures *immediately subsequent* tasks; "put users in episodic-retrieval mode for the whole session" (RESEARCH_NOTES §16) extrapolates duration and materials beyond the paradigm.

### P2-5 — Reminiscence-bump framing extrapolates beyond the population studied
Koppel & Rubin 2016 is real and its cue-dependence finding is characterized accurately (verified: word cues → smaller/earlier bump; important-memory requests → later, life-script events). But the bump is a phenomenon documented in adults roughly 40+ recalling their youth; residency applicants are mostly 25–35, where recency dominates the retrieval distribution. The design conclusion (cue school/childhood/first-job territories; avoid "most important" phrasing) survives on independent grounds — cue-driven retrieval and life-script avoidance — so this is a citation-hygiene nuance, not a broken inference. A one-line caveat in RESEARCH_NOTES §14 would fix it.

### P2-6 — Back-then-forward silently destroys a typed draft and swaps the question
`b1513WizardBack()` preserves selections and (correctly) keeps `insp.answer`. But returning forward re-runs `b1513WizardPick('energy', …)` → `b1513NextPrompt()`, which clears `insp.answer` and serves a *different* prompt (the old one is already in `seenIds`). A student who typed half an answer, tapped Back to reconsider the energy, and stepped forward again loses their text with no warning — against the standing agency copy "your progress is saved" and the §3 "going back never loses selections" spirit (selections survive; work does not). Fix: if `insp.answer` is non-empty, returning to the question step should restore the same prompt and draft (or auto-save-for-later).

### P2-7 — Admin "+ Add question" bypasses every completeness and safety rule the library sets for itself
`extensions.js` line ~1193: a new prompt is created as `{id, text, who:['you'], domain:['personal'], energy:['light'], state:'active'}` from a single `window.prompt()` — **no `followUp`, no `interviewUse`, no territory, no safety/IMG screen**, live immediately. The appendix's own extension method (§7: complete all fields, write exits into sensitive text, run the IMG screen) is unenforceable in the only authoring pathway the prototype offers. Governance depth is declared R4 (Doc 09), but the R3 prototype pathway ships a door around the checklist; at minimum the prototype should refuse to activate a prompt with missing fields, since production is told to copy this reference.

### P2-8 — Demo-seed provenance mismatch
`shim.js` line 238: seeded story origin is `{promptId: 'q-034', promptText: 'Who in your family taught you something without ever calling it teaching?'}` — but the bank's q-034 is the unexpected-thank-you prompt. Snapshot semantics make this harmless at runtime, but the seed silently demonstrates an id/text divergence that reviewers may mistake for intended behavior. (Same file, the saved-item fallback text "What's the first meal you learned to cook for yourself?" is a phantom prompt that exists nowhere in the bank.)

## Bottom line

The evidence base is honest and the cueing design will genuinely broaden recall — the territories, cue concreteness, who-dimension, and ramp are faithful applications of real research, and the safety engineering is the strongest part of the work. The open risk to the product's core promise is not the questions but the *last mile*: as implemented, a typed answer never receives its follow-up probe (P1-1), and nothing at answer time pushes the surfaced memory toward stakes and change or tells the student why it matters (P1-2). Fix those two and the pleasant-reminiscence risk largely disappears; ship as-is and the wizard will surface wonderful material that a distracted student never converts.
