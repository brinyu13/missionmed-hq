## 1. Methodology

Research was conducted by web investigation on 2026-08-07 across nine literatures chosen to triangulate the problem from independent directions: narrative medicine, reflective medical education, oral history and folklife practice, life-story and qualitative interviewing, autobiographical-memory science, behavioral interviewing, memoir/storytelling craft, human-centered design interviewing, and closeness-generating conversation research.

Verification and selection standards:

- **Corroboration.** Key claims were verified across at least two independent sources where possible (e.g., "structure beats blank pages" holds in both the BMC scoping review and the AMEE guide; "warm up before depth" converges independently from Aron's experimental escalation, StoryCorps practice, and Baylor's rapport-first training).
- **Credibility screen.** Sources are official bodies (AAMC, AMA, Library of Congress, Smithsonian), peer-reviewed research programs (McAdams, Rubin, Schacter, Singer, Raes, Aron), canonical practitioner texts (Janz; Fisher & Geiselman), or standard-setting practice organizations (StoryCorps, The Moth, IDEO.org). Each entry in the source table carries a one-line credibility statement.
- **Copyright hygiene.** No proprietary question bank was copied, in whole or in part. Only method-level principles were extracted from practice sources. Every source is tagged with a provenance level (below), and the tag is a claim MissionMed stands behind per source.

Provenance vocabulary (fixed):

- **adapted** — MissionMed content applies a method-level *structure* from the source (never verbatim text or question lists).
- **inspired** — the source's findings or philosophy informed design choices; no structural borrowing.
- **independently authored (context only)** — the source establishes the target use case; no content was derived from it.

## 4. Originality statement

All 81 questions in `PROMPT_LIBRARY.json` are MissionMed-original text. No question list from StoryCorps, McAdams/Foley Center, Aron et al., The Moth, Smithsonian, Baylor, the Library of Congress, or any other source was copied in whole or in part. StoryCorps and McAdams — the two sources with famous public question sets — were used for **method principles only**: StoryCorps for phrasing style, warm-up-first ordering, and follow-up pattern; McAdams for the scene-detail scaffold applied inside `followUp` probes. The four *adapted* sources (Smithsonian territory mapping, Baylor probe design and one-question flow, Crovitz cue-word logic, Fisher–Geiselman context reinstatement) contributed structures, each attributed in §2; the text realizing those structures was authored fresh for MissionMed's population and register. The two *independently authored (context only)* sources (AAMC, AMA) define what a usable output story must contain — a specific moment plus change/insight, feeding behavioral answers — which the `interviewUse` field encodes; nothing was derived from their content.

## 6. Consciously-left gaps (safety by design)

These are decisions, not omissions; they implement P9 and the spine's no-trauma-pressure rule:

- **No prompts demanding grief, loss, medical error, or discrimination narratives.** Students who want to volunteer such stories can do so through open `moving` invitations (e.g., q-014's goodbye prompt), each of which carries an explicit exit in its own text. The wizard will never be the thing that asked.
- **No dedicated religion/spirituality territory.** Reachable through culture/tradition/celebration prompts on the student's own terms; applicant comfort varies too widely, and interviewers rarely probe it.
- **Romantic relationships limited and mild.** Confined to the `spouse_partner` whoDetail; dating stories are rarely interview-usable.
- **No "greatest achievement / biggest weakness" prompts.** Deliberately excluded as life-script bait (P1, P6, source 14): the wizard exists precisely for the material such questions fail to surface.

## 7. How to extend the library (method note for future MissionMed authors)

1. **Start from a cell or territory, not from a question you like.** Check §5 coverage first; extend thin cells (family × medical_clinical, someone_else × academic) or add a territory that supplies a new concrete cue class.
2. **Author against the principles.** The question must cue one particular episode (P1) with a concrete, imageable anchor (P8), be answerable as a story rather than an opinion (P14), and stay a single open invitation — never two questions in one (P2).
3. **Write the exits into the text** for any prompt approaching sensitive ground ("Share only what feels comfortable"), require de-identification inside any patient prompt, and never demand trauma, grief, error, or discrimination disclosure (P9, §6).
4. **Run the IMG screen.** No US-centric cultural anchors; migration framed as opportunity; no assumed family structure, religion, or country of training.
5. **Complete all fields.** `followUp` applies the scene scaffold (place → senses → thought/feeling → meaning, P7) and must not introduce a new topic; `interviewUse` must name, in one sentence, the stakes/change material the prompt yields and the interview or personal-statement question it feeds (P10); assign `territory` and every honestly applicable dimension tag.
6. **Tag honestly, not aspirationally.** Multi-tagging (`who`, `energy`) is allowed only when the prompt genuinely reads correctly on each path; recompute coverage counts after adding.
7. **Publish through governance, never code.** New prompts enter via the versioned `sf_inspiration_prompts` configuration domain (spine D6; Doc 09) with stable ids (`q-XXX`), audited publish, and `retired` — never deletion — for withdrawal, so saved snapshots and story provenance stay intact.
8. **Provenance discipline.** New prompts must remain MissionMed-original text. If a new *method* source is used, add it to `RESEARCH_NOTES.md` with a credibility line and a provenance tag before the prompts it informs are published.
