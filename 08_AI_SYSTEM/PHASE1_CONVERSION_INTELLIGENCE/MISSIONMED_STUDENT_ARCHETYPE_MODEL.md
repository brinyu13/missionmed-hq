## Archetype 1: Price-Sensitive Applicant

### Profile
The largest archetype (47% of all threads). These students are interested in the program but cost is their primary or dominant concern. They often ask about pricing early in the conversation and may go silent after receiving cost information.

**Typical characteristics:**
- Lead with cost questions ("How much does it cost?", "Is there a payment plan?")
- May express interest before asking about price, or price is their first question
- Often compare value vs. cost rather than rejecting outright
- Decision timeline: variable — some decide quickly once given installment terms, others stall

**Common objections:** Price (primary), sometimes combined with timeline or visa concerns

**Typical decision latency:** Variable — fast when installment terms are provided, stalls when only the total price is given

**Conversion rate:** 5.7% (8/141) — the lowest conversion rate, but the largest volume

### Best Persuasion Strategy
Lead with **financial flexibility** — specific installment terms, payment schedules, deposit amounts. Do not lead with value justification. The student asking about price has already decided the program has value; they need to know they can afford it.

Follow with **outcome reference** — a brief statement connecting the financial investment to the career outcome (matching into residency). Frame as investment, not cost.

### Recommended Email Tone
Direct, practical, supportive. Avoid being apologetic about pricing or defensive. Treat the question as a logistics question, not an objection.

### Recommended Call Strategy
Suggest a call if the student doesn't respond within 48 hours of receiving payment information. The call allows for personalized financial discussion that's harder via email.

## Archetype 2: Standard Inquirer

### Profile
The second largest group (20%). These students are exploring the program — they've heard about Mission Residency and want to learn more. They don't arrive with a strong emotional driver or specific concern.

**Typical characteristics:**
- Opening email is general: "I'm interested in your program" or "Can you tell me more?"
- Ask broad questions before narrowing to specifics
- May or may not have a specific concern
- Low urgency — exploring options

**Common objections:** None initially; may develop price or timeline concerns later

**Typical decision latency:** Medium — requires 2–4 exchanges to build enough engagement

**Conversion rate:** 13.6% (8/59) — moderate

### Best Persuasion Strategy
Provide a **comprehensive first response** that answers the likely questions before they're asked: program overview, outcomes, session dates, and cost — all in one email. End with a specific question back to the student to keep the conversation moving.

Add **social proof early** — standard inquirers need a reason to choose this program over alternatives. A testimonial or success reference in the first email differentiates from a generic information packet.

### Recommended Email Tone
Warm, informative, professional. Match their exploratory energy with enthusiasm about the program.

### Recommended Call Strategy
Offer a call in the second email if the student's first reply shows engagement. Standard inquirers often need a human conversation to move from "interested" to "committed."

## Archetype 3: Late-Cycle Panic Applicant

### Profile
Students who reach out close to a deadline, after match results, or when they realize they need help urgently. High emotional energy, often ready to act quickly once reassured.

**Typical characteristics:**
- Language includes urgency: "Is it too late?", "The deadline is coming up", "I just found out I didn't match"
- Emotionally charged — anxiety mixed with determination
- Longer threads (avg 6.2 emails) because the urgency drives active engagement
- Responsive — reply quickly to Michelle's emails

**Common objections:** Timeline (primary), sometimes combined with price or scores

**Typical decision latency:** 1.1 days — fast once reassured that it's not too late

**Conversion rate:** 33.3% (17/51) — **highest conversion rate among large archetypes**

### Best Persuasion Strategy
**Speed is everything.** These students need to hear "Yes, there's still time" within hours, not days. Respond immediately with availability confirmation and enrollment steps.

Add **urgency framing** (when factually accurate): "Spots are limited for this session." This channels their anxiety into action rather than paralysis.

### Recommended Email Tone
Urgent but calm. Acknowledge their concern, confirm availability, and provide an immediate action path. Don't add friction — these students are ready to move.

### Recommended Call Strategy
Suggest a call immediately if the first email shows high anxiety. A voice conversation can de-escalate panic and close the enrollment in real-time.

## Archetype 4: Fast Decisive Enroller

### Profile
Students who arrive ready to enroll. Minimal questions, minimal objections. They've already made their decision before contacting Mission Residency.

**Typical characteristics:**
- First email is logistical: "How do I enroll?", "Where do I send payment?", "What documents do I need?"
- 2–3 emails total
- Often referred by another student or alumnus
- May already have program knowledge from website, social media, or word of mouth

**Common objections:** None (or minor logistical questions)

**Typical decision latency:** Near-instant — commitment signal in first or second email

**Conversion rate:** 100% (35/35) — by definition, these are confirmed enrollers

### Best Persuasion Strategy
Don't over-sell. Provide **enrollment instructions clearly and immediately**. Any unnecessary information (lengthy program descriptions, extensive testimonials) adds friction that isn't needed.

### Recommended Email Tone
Efficient, warm, welcoming. Match their directness. "Great to have you! Here's exactly how to get started."

### Recommended Call Strategy
Not needed unless the student requests one. These students prefer to self-serve.

## Archetype 5: IMG Standard

### Profile
International Medical Graduates who need to confirm the program works for their situation. Their primary question is eligibility and program relevance to IMG pathways.

**Typical characteristics:**
- Ask about ECFMG, visa, credential requirements
- Want to know if the program specifically helps IMGs
- May ask about success rates for IMGs specifically
- Moderate engagement — 4–5 email average

**Common objections:** Visa/IMG eligibility (primary), sometimes price

**Conversion rate:** 40.0% (2/5) — strong, but small sample

### Best Persuasion Strategy
Lead with **explicit IMG inclusion**: "Yes, our program is designed for IMGs. Here's how it works for your pathway." Follow with **IMG-specific outcome references** — success stories of IMGs who matched through the program.

### Recommended Email Tone
Informative, authoritative, reassuring. IMGs need to trust that the program understands their specific regulatory challenges.

### Recommended Call Strategy
Offer a call to discuss their specific credential situation. IMG pathways are complex and individual — email may not cover all nuances.

## Archetype 6: Analytical IMG Researcher

### Profile
A subset of IMG students who approach the decision analytically. They research extensively, ask detailed questions, compare programs, and take longer to decide.

**Typical characteristics:**
- Ask about match rates, outcome data, specific curriculum details
- May compare Mission Residency to other programs
- Longer threads (avg 9.0 emails) — they need comprehensive information
- Methodical — each email advances their research

**Common objections:** Legitimacy, visa/IMG, sometimes price

**Conversion rate:** 20.0% (1/5) — moderate, small sample

### Best Persuasion Strategy
Provide **data and specifics** — match rates, outcome statistics, detailed program structure. Don't rely on emotional appeals; these students respond to evidence.

Add **testimonials from IMGs** who were similarly analytical — "Dr [Anonymous] spent several weeks researching before enrolling, and ultimately matched into [specialty]."

### Recommended Email Tone
Professional, data-driven, thorough. Don't rush them — match their pace. Answer every question completely.

### Recommended Call Strategy
Offer a call after the 3rd or 4th email. Position it as a chance to get detailed answers: "I'd love to walk you through our outcome data — would a quick call be helpful?"

## Archetype 7: Cautious Slow Decider

### Profile
Students who engage over many emails but never reach a commitment. They're interested but paralyzed by indecision, risk aversion, or information overload.

**Typical characteristics:**
- 8+ email threads
- Ask many questions but don't progress toward enrollment
- May revisit previously-answered questions
- Language suggests overthinking: "I need to think about it more," "I'm not sure yet"

**Common objections:** Multiple — may raise price, timing, AND legitimacy concerns across different emails

**Conversion rate:** 0% (0/4) — the hardest archetype to convert

### Best Persuasion Strategy
**Simplify the decision.** Reduce the number of options, provide a clear recommended path, and set a gentle deadline: "I'd recommend the upcoming session — it starts [date] and would be a great fit for your situation."

**Break the email loop** by suggesting a phone call. These students need a human conversation to break through analysis paralysis.

### Recommended Email Tone
Patient, structured, decisive. Don't add more information — simplify. Provide a clear recommendation rather than options.

### Recommended Call Strategy
Essential. After 4+ emails without progression, a phone call is the most effective intervention. "Let's hop on a quick call — I can help you figure out the best path for your specific situation."

## Archetype 8: Anxious General

### Profile
Students experiencing significant emotional distress about their career prospects. Their anxiety may relate to prior failures, personal circumstances, or general career uncertainty.

**Typical characteristics:**
- Emotionally loaded language: scared, afraid, overwhelmed, hopeless
- Longer threads (11+ emails) — need extensive emotional support
- May need multiple reassurance cycles before feeling confident enough to act
- The decision to enroll is not just financial — it's emotional

**Common objections:** Personal anxiety (primary), may combine with scores or unmatched status

**Conversion rate:** Low — these students are the most challenging to convert via email

### Best Persuasion Strategy
Lead with **empathy and validation**: "I completely understand how you're feeling. What you're going through is something many medical graduates experience." Follow with **social proof from similar students** who felt the same way and ultimately succeeded.

Do NOT push enrollment early. Build trust and confidence first over several emails.

### Recommended Email Tone
Warm, supportive, patient, human. This is where Michelle's natural empathetic communication style is most valuable. The tone should feel like a conversation with a supportive mentor, not a salesperson.

### Recommended Call Strategy
Suggest a call early and gently. "I'd love to talk to you about this — sometimes it helps to hear a friendly voice. Would you be open to a quick chat?" The phone call is the single most important intervention for this archetype.

## Appendix: Methodology

Archetype classification uses a rule-based system analyzing emotional language patterns, objection types, thread length, response speed, and conversion status. Students are assigned to the highest-priority matching archetype (e.g., an anxious student with IMG concerns who has low scores would be classified based on the most distinctive combination of signals). Archetype boundaries are not rigid — some students may exhibit traits of multiple archetypes across a conversation. The model is designed for practical response routing rather than academic precision.
